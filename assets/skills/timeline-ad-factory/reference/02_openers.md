# 02: Openers

"Here's what happens when..." is ONE door. The milestones, ladder, mechanism and close underneath never change. The opener decides WHO tells it, in what tense, with what emotional license. Swapping only the opener is the cheapest test there is: RYZE ran the same 290-word body behind a Testimony opener and a Hypothetical opener.

Slots: [AVATAR] [PAIN] [PRODUCT] [WHO/AUTHORITY] [RITUAL] [TIMELINE] [OTHER]. Fill them with the variables from Stage 1. Be SPECIFIC: "two [PRODUCT] softgels before every shift" beats "a pill"; "a nurse on the emergency floor for 12 years" beats "a nurse".

The example lines below use one made-up product (a turmeric joint softgel) so you can see the opener change while the body holds. Real "in the wild" lines are quoted as they ran.

## The 16 openers

**1. Hypothetical.** "This is what WOULD happen to [AVATAR] with [PAIN] if he [RITUAL] for [TIMELINE]."
In the wild (RYZE): "This is what would happen to a burnt-out blue-collar dad if he replaced his energy drinks."
Why: future-pacing with deniability; a what-if makes no promise. Use: cold traffic, no real customer story.

**2. Testimony.** "A [AVATAR] [swapped X for RITUAL] for [TIMELINE]. Here's what happened."
In the wild (RYZE): "A construction worker replaced his energy drinks with mushroom coffee for 30 days. Here's what happened."
Why: reads like a report of something that already happened. Use: a real or representative case.

**3. Diary.** "I've been [WHO/AUTHORITY]. [PAIN]. Here's what happened once I started [RITUAL]."
Example: "I've been a nurse on the emergency floor for 12 years. By the end of every shift, my knees were screaming. Here's what happened once I started taking two [PRODUCT] softgels before every shift."
Why: first person, one voice; the WHO doubles as authority. Use: emotional payoffs.

**4. Skeptic.** "I bought [PRODUCT] to EXPOSE it. [Every X I tried did nothing], so I documented [TIMELINE] to prove it."
In the wild (Resilia): "I bought Resilia to expose it. I set up a camera and documented 30 days to prove it does nothing."
Why: the narrator IS the objection; every milestone is a grudging concession. Use: jaded markets.

**5. Bro / Yapper Friend.** "What happens if [your buddy who PAIN] actually [RITUAL] every day?"
In the wild (Resilia): "What happens if your bro actually takes oregano oil every day? Day 1, nothing happens. He calls you to say it's a scam. Classic bro."
Why: comedy; the skeptic beat lands on a character they laugh at. Use: younger or male audiences.

**6. Witness.** "Here's what happened when [OTHER: my dad], who [PAIN], finally [RITUAL]."
Tell it in the PAST tense; the narrator watched it already happen.
Why: the narrator IS the witness. Use: gift buyers, spouses, adult kids buying for parents.

**7. False Warning.** "If you [PAIN/TRAIT], be careful taking [PRODUCT]. Because here's what could happen..."
In the wild (RYZE): "If you smoke weed, you gotta be super careful if you drink RYZE. Because here's what could happen."
Why: framed as a downside, the desire can be stated far more vividly. Use: wants they'd never admit out loud.

**8. Talking Problem.** "I'm [PAIN, personified]. Here's what happens to me when you start [RITUAL]."
In the wild (RYZE): "I'm a 42 year old mom bod. This is what happens to me after drinking one cup every day. Day one, I don't feel shit."
Why: the problem narrates its own retirement. Use: embarrassing problems.

**9. Harm Ladder.** "This is what might happen if you keep [ignoring PAIN] for too long. After [Year 1]... by [Year 3]..."
In the wild (RYZE): "This is what might happen to a man if he smoked weed every single night for too long. After 12 months... by year 3... by year 5..."
Why: damage climbs, then a concession, then the pivot to the product. Use: habits they won't quit, compliance-sensitive products.

**10. Two Paths.** "Two [PRODUCTS]. One of them you'll take for [TIMELINE] while [PAIN keeps going]. Pick."
In the wild (Resilia): "Two oregano oil supplements. One of them you'll take every day for eight months while the parasites keep multiplying. Pick."
Why: the failed fix gets the harm ladder, yours gets the recovery ladder. Use: they tried the cheap version and quit.

**11. Conditional Ladder.** "If you take [PRODUCT] for just [one week], you might notice... If you take it for [two weeks]..."
Why: pure benefit ladder, no avatar. Use: retargeting, product-aware, lapsed buyers.

**12. Situation Open.** "[NAME]'s [SITUATION] every [WHEN]. [WHO ELSE IT HURTS]. Then [PRODUCT] showed up."
In the wild (Aurivita): "Frank's feet were ice cold in bed every night. She'd yelp and pull away... Then a little package showed up."
Full structure: situation -> product arrives (blind is spicier) -> short timeline (tiny win, self-observation, witness) -> real cause ("It was never his age") -> failed fixes -> reveal + mechanism picture -> ritual + safety net -> offer.

**13. Head-to-Head.** "Two [AVATARS] got [the same bad news]. One took [the usual fix]. One took [PRODUCT]." Then week by week, side by side.
Why: the failed fix is built INTO the timeline. Use: a popular "usual fix" you beat.

**14. Spiral, Then Rescue.** "POV: you're [AGE] and [the scary moment]." Days get WORSE, then the product, then they get better.
Why: a negative and a positive timeline in one ad. Use: problems that get scarier when ignored.

**15. Ingredient Experiment.** "What would happen if you [ate the raw ingredient] every day for [TIMELINE]?" Then the product gets the same result without the downside.
Why: curiosity about a kitchen ingredient they already trust. Use: the hero ingredient is a familiar food or spice.

**16. "Don't Buy".** "Don't buy [PRODUCT] if [you like having an excuse to PAIN]."
In the wild (RYZE, 149 days running): "Do not buy RYZE Mushroom Coffee."
Why: sounds like it works against the sale, so they need to know why.

## The Borrowed Open

The opener can come from ANY winning ad in any format. Take a proven situation, frustration or micro-moment, add ONE bridge line, then hit Day 1 fast. Benchmark: RYZE's #12-ranked timeline ad says "Day one" 53 words in.

Bridge lines:
- "That was me. Until my [PERSON] handed me [PRODUCT]. Day one..."
- "So I tried one thing for 30 days. Here's what happened."
- "Then she started [RITUAL]. Week one..."
- "Here's what changed when I stopped [FAILED FIX] and started [RITUAL]."
