# 00: Hook elements (the highest-leverage part of the ad)

Almost every timeline ad opens on one line, and that line is built from a handful of elements. Each element can be described a dozen different ways, and every different description is a new hook to test on the SAME ad body. Your job in Stage 1 is to generate MANY options for each element, then combine them into a big batch of opening lines.

The base shape:
"Here's what happens when [SEGMENT] with [PAIN POINT] [CONSUMPTION VERB] [SOLUTION] for [TIMELINE]."

Same product, three very different hooks:
- "Here's what happens when you take RYZE mushroom coffee for 30 days." (named)
- "Here's what happens when weed smokers drink this delicious chocolaty latte every morning." (flavor)
- "Here's what happens when weed smokers chug this weird brown beverage every morning." (blind, curiosity)

## Element 1: The segment (WHO, and how you describe them)

Pinpoint exactly who the ad talks to, then find several ways to say it. Pull these from the input (sales page, reviews, the winning ad's callouts). Angles to mine:
- Age + life stage: "women over 45", "new dads", "retirees"
- Job / daily grind: "nurses on 12-hour shifts", "truck drivers", "teachers"
- Habit or identity: "weed smokers", "lifelong coffee addicts", "gym rats over 40"
- Condition, in plain words: "diabetic men", "guys with bad knees"
- Situation: "moms who haven't slept since the baby", "men who snore so loud their wife moved rooms"
Give at least 6 segments, each with 2-3 ways to describe it. More specific almost always beats more general.

## Element 2: The pain point (and how you describe it)

The same pain can be described clinically, physically, emotionally or through a situation:
- "joint pain" / "creaky knees" / "knees that sound like bubble wrap" / "grabbing the railing on every stair"
- "low energy" / "the 3pm crash" / "falling asleep on the couch by 8"
Give 2-3 pain points with 3+ descriptions each. The situational version (a moment you can SEE) is usually strongest.

## Element 3: The consumption verb (how they use it)

The verb sets the picture and the tone. Match it to the product AND to the energy of the segment:
- Neutral: takes, drinks, uses, applies
- Ritual: sips every morning, stirs into their coffee, swallows two before bed, sprinkles on their food
- Vivid / playful: chugs, slams, pops, rubs on, dabs, gulps
Give 5+ verbs that fit the product.

## Element 4: The solution (named, general, or blind)

Three ways to describe the product. Pick per hook; test all three.

**Named:** the brand or product name. Best when the brand already has recognition, for retargeting, or when the name itself is curious. "RYZE mushroom coffee", "Aurivita cayenne softgels".

**General (category):** the category people already know. Best when the category is trusted and familiar. "mushroom coffee", "a cayenne supplement", "a magnesium drink".

**Blind (curiosity):** describe it without naming it, so they have to keep watching to find out what it is. Ways to build a blind characterization:
- **Color:** "this weird brown beverage", "a red powder", "two little red softgels"
- **Texture / form:** "a white paste", "a gritty powder", "this thick golden oil"
- **Flavor:** "a chocolaty latte", "a bitter tea", "a spicy pepper"
- **Weird or exotic origin:** "a mushroom that grows on dying trees", "a root Japanese monks used", "a resin from the mountains"
- **Comparison to something familiar:** "a fruit", "a sugar", "a spice already in your kitchen", "a kitchen ingredient", when one of the main ingredients genuinely IS that (only if it's true)
- **Mystery package:** "a little package", "a weird bottle my sister sent me"
Give at least 3 named/general options and 6+ blind options. The blind description must be TRUE to the product.

## Element 5: The timeline

- **In the hook, start short:** 14 or 30 days. A short window feels believable and easy to commit to. (Longer works for slow categories, but default short.)
- **In the body, let the results keep going.** Even if the hook says 14 or 30 days, carry the milestones out to Month 2 and Month 3, with results that COMPOUND (they keep getting better). See the AOV trick in `03_script_rules.md`.
Give 3-4 timeline options for the hook (7 days, 14 days, 30 days, 60 days).

## Element 6: The opener type

The same elements can go through any of the 16 doors in `02_openers.md` (Hypothetical, Testimony, Diary, Situation Open, Two Paths...). Use several.

## Stage 1 output: the Hook Bank for this product

1. **The element menu:** a table per element with all the options above.
2. **20+ opening lines** that combine the elements in different ways. Mix named, general and blind solutions, at least 5 opener types, and several segments. Number them. Label each with its opener type and solution mode (named/general/blind).
3. **Your top 5 picks** and why, by market fit.

The user picks one or more lines (or mixes elements). That pick becomes the frame for the script. The other lines are saved to `hooks.md` in the project folder as ready-made hook variations to test on the same ad body later.
