# 01: Elements (Trigger Hunting)

Every top-spending ad, in ANY format, is proof of what works on its market right now. Trigger Hunting = pulling the proven variables out of a winner (or a brief, or a sales page) and loading them into a timeline ad. The timeline is the container. The input tells you what goes in it.

## The 10 variables

Extract every one you can find. Quote the market's own words where possible. Leave a variable blank rather than invent it.

| Variable | What to look for | Where it goes in the timeline ad |
|---|---|---|
| **Segment** | Who it calls out: "men over 50", "diabetic dads", "nurses on night shift" | The frame / hook |
| **Situation** | ONE specific moment where the problem fires: a place, a time, an object, often someone else watching | The opener |
| **Pain points** | Symptoms and frustrations, in their words | The before-state and Day 1 |
| **Objections** | What stops them buying: "I've tried everything", "it's just my age", "it's in my head" | The skeptic beat, and one way to mine milestones |
| **Wrong suspect + real villain** | What they blame vs. what's actually causing it | The real-cause beat ("It was never his age. It was...") |
| **Failed fixes** | What they already tried | After the timeline, or built into it (Head-to-Head, Two Paths) |
| **Desired outcome** | The result they crave, in their words | The top of the milestone ladder |
| **Witness** | Who notices: spouse, kid, coworker, doctor | The social-observation milestone |
| **Identity payoff** | Who they get to be again, how they're seen | The final scene |
| **Mechanism picture** | The one simple image that explains why it works ("Sugar chokes the little pipes. Cayenne helps keep them flowing.") | Ingredient credits inside the timeline, or the explanation after it |

## What makes a killer SITUATION

A situation beats a symptom every time. "Cold feet" is a symptom. "His wife yelps and pulls away in bed, and now she sleeps on the far edge" is a situation. A strong one has most of:
- **A body in the room:** a real person, a real place, a real time.
- **A physical detail you can SEE:** an object, a sound, a sensation.
- **Somebody else it hurts:** a partner, a kid, a coworker.
- **A cost bigger than the symptom:** what they're really losing.

## Reading each input type

**Brief:** pull what's given, then ASK the user for the missing high-value variables (especially the situation, the witness and the failed fixes). Offer 3 guesses for each so they can just pick.

**Sales page:** sales pages are packed with variables. The headline and lead usually hold the segment and outcome. The "story" section holds situations and the wrong suspect. The FAQ holds objections. The mechanism section holds the villain and the mechanism picture. Testimonials hold witnesses and identity payoffs, in real customer words.

**Transcript of another winning ad (any format):** this is the best input. Pull the situation, the pain points and the witness word for word. Note which variables the ad leans on hardest; that's likely why it's spending. **Borrow the moment, never the claim:** situations and pain points transfer to the user's product; another brand's health claims and mechanism do not.

## Milestones come from two places

1. **What they WANT:** outcomes and benefits. Climb them in ladder order.
2. **What stops them:** objections. List them in the order they'd hit the prospect and give each one the milestone where it dies:

| Objection | Milestone that kills it |
|---|---|
| "I've tried stuff like this, nothing works" | Day 1 / Week 1 skeptic beat: agree with them first ("Week one, nothing.") |
| "It's probably in my head" | A small BEHAVIOR win they can't argue with |
| "It won't fix the thing I actually care about" | The visible win inside the exact situation they hate |
| "Nobody will notice anyway" | The witness |
| "Why would this work when everything else failed?" | After the timeline: real cause, failed fixes, mechanism picture |
